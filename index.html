AI Analysis — July 2026
May & June shown as trend reference

July's dispute volume reflects an ATO incident on physical cards in Mexico (Tutuka), with 80.5% of 220 disputes still in progress — a pending-review cohort, not confirmed loss, as BPS fell to 0.54.

Disputes opened fell in absolute terms from 310 in June and 588 in May, but the share of tickets converting dropped sharply to 20.1% from June's 30.9% and May's 64.4%. The composition, however, is the real story: 'transaction not recognised' now dominates at 1,034 cases (94.4%), consistent with an ATO fraud pattern rather than merchant service disputes. This concentration, alongside the physical-card and Tutuka signals, points to the Mexico incident as the primary volume driver. The operational consequence is a fraud-heavy caseload that is quick to file but slow to resolve, front-loading workload into evidence submission over the next 45–120 days.
In-progress cases surged to 177 (80.5% of disputes) from just 73 (23.5%) in June and 18 (3.1%) in May — an absolute jump of 104 cases versus June. This is expected: the ATO cohort was filed recently and sits well inside the 45–120 day network resolution window. The 1.4% win rate (3 cases) is therefore not alarming — it reflects an immature cohort, not poor performance, since nearly all outcomes are simply pending. The consequence is that July's true win/refund/loss profile cannot be judged yet and will only crystallise across August–October as presentments resolve.
Of $84,166 in credits issued, $71,925 (85.5%) is classified as to-be-recovered — still at risk but not yet realized. This contrasts with June, where $102,093 became confirmed loss on a larger $198,555 credit base, and May's near-clean $651 loss. Confirmed loss in July is comparatively contained at $11,166 (13.3% of credits), reinforcing that this month is exposure-in-motion, not booked cost. The consequence: recovery outcomes on this at-risk pool will define July's real financial impact, and provisional credits on ATO fraud cases carry meaningful recoverability given strong 'not recognised' evidence.
Overall card mix shows physical at 268 (24.5%) versus virtual 827 (75.5%), but the direction of change matters: physical share sat at 76.6% in June and 86.4% in May, so the aggregate has shifted toward virtual even as physical volume remains the flagged vector. The ATO incident specifically targeted physical cards in Mexico on Tutuka, and the processor mix corroborates this — tutuka and tutukaColombia together account for 294 cases (26.9%), with tutukaBrazil dominant at 792. These signals converge on a single physical-card fraud driver in the Mexico Tutuka footprint. The consequence is a concentrated remediation focus: reissuance and controls on the affected physical card population rather than a broad portfolio issue.
BPS collapsed to 0.54 from 8.59 in June and 7.76 in May — an 8.05-point drop that pulls Jeeves well below the 5 bps healthy threshold and far from the 8 bps processor-review trigger. This means disputed amount as a fraction of transaction volume is now very low, despite elevated case counts, indicating the ATO disputes are individually small relative to total Tutuka volume. The consequence is reduced processor-review risk even amid a fraud incident. This is a genuinely healthy signal and should be watched to ensure it holds as the ATO cohort's amounts finalise.
Fraud lens: The fraud signals converge cleanly this month: 'transaction not recognised' at 94.4% (1,034 cases), a physical-card vector concentrated in the Mexico Tutuka footprint, and 3DS coverage that is effectively absent for disputed flows — only 9 cases (0.8%) carried a 3DS signal while 432 (39.5%) show no signal and 653 (59.6%) MDES. Together these are consistent with an Account Takeover on physical cards rather than merchant-dispute noise, though we should not assert an exact count at the three-way intersection. Critically, part of this elevated volume reflects Jeeves' own detection working: compromised transactions were surfaced and formally disputed quickly, which is fraud being caught and acted on, not simply fraud rising unchecked. The low BPS of 0.54 alongside high case counts supports this reading — many small-value fraudulent charges identified and filed. The near-total dominance of 'not recognised' also argues against structural merchant issues and toward a discrete, containable compromise event.
Watch: Track resolution of the 177 in-progress disputes as they exit the 45–120 day window; if the win-plus-refund rate on the matured ATO cohort settles below roughly 60% (versus June's 74.5% combined), escalate evidence quality on physical-card fraud filings. Monitor tutuka and tutukaColombia BPS monthly — a rebound toward 8 bps would signal the incident is recurring rather than contained. Confirm reissuance and controls on affected Mexico physical cards have closed the vector, using next month's physical-card 'not recognised' volume as the trigger.

Section 8 · Glossary
Key terms explained
Chargeback
A forced reversal of a card transaction initiated by the card network on behalf of the cardholder. The merchant is debited and the issuer (Jeeves) recovers funds.
Win Rate
Percentage of filed disputes where the card network ruled in our favour. Calculated over opened disputes only — not over all tickets.
Temporary Credit
A provisional credit given to the client while the dispute is pending. Reversed if the dispute is lost; becomes permanent if won.
Financial Loss
A credit issued on a case that ended in a non-winning status (lost, not eligible, stopped, below $15). Confirmed cost to Jeeves.
BPS (Basis Points)
Disputed amount as a fraction of total transaction volume, × 10,000. E.g., 5 bps = 0.05% of volume disputed. Processors monitor this metric.
2nd Presentment
A second round of the dispute filed after reviewing the merchant's evidence rebuttal. Last chance before Pre-Arbitration.
Pre-Arbitration
An escalation stage where the card network acts as arbitrator. Higher fees apply. Used when both parties maintain their position after 2nd presentment.
3DS (3D Secure)
Authentication protocol (Verified by Visa / Mastercard SecureCode). If authenticated, fraud liability shifts to the merchant — making chargebacks very hard to file.
Not Eligible
Transaction cannot be disputed via the card network — often because it exceeded the 120-day dispute window or falls under a non-disputable transaction type.
MCC (Merchant Category Code)
4-digit code assigned by card networks to classify merchant type. E.g., 4121 = Taxicabs/Rideshare, 7372 = Software/SaaS. Used to spot dispute concentration by industry.
Stopped
Dispute was intentionally not continued after reviewing merchant evidence — typically when the evidence was compelling enough to justify stopping the process.
Refunded by Merchant
Merchant returned funds voluntarily (without a network ruling). Faster than a chargeback win and avoids dispute fees. Counted as a recovery.
